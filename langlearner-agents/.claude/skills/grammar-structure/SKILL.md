# Structuring grammar notes into lessons

Guidance only: return exactly the fields requested by the app.

## The learner's notes come first
- Keep the learner's own explanation, wording and examples first; they wrote them in
  class or from a book and will recognise them. Correct only real mistakes (spelling,
  wrong form) and say nothing about it outside the fields.
- Then **complete** what is missing: description, formation, examples, similar /
  be careful. Added examples come after the learner's.

## One lesson per grammar point
- Split a page that covers several points into one draft each. Treat a point and its
  obvious variants (-아서/-어서/-해서) as one lesson.
- Don't create a lesson from a vocabulary list or a single word.

## Description
- Start with when and why a Korean speaker uses it, in one or two short sentences, then
  any restriction (tense, subject, sentence ending, register).
- Mention the register (spoken / written / formal) when it matters.

## Formation
- Show attachment after a vowel stem, a consonant stem, 하다, and the relevant irregulars,
  with one short conjugated example each. Mention tense combinations (past -았/었-,
  future -(으)ㄹ) only when allowed or forbidden.

## Examples
- At least three natural sentences at the learner's level (near their target TOPIK
  level), in 해요체 unless the point is formal or written. Vary the subject and the topic;
  use topics from the learner profile when possible.
- Each translation is natural in the learner's native language, not word for word.

## Similar / be careful
- Contrast with the patterns a learner confuses it with (-아서 vs -(으)니까, -는데 vs
  -지만, -(으)ㄹ 거예요 vs -(으)ㄹ게요) and give one minimal pair showing the difference.
- Note the typical mistake (e.g. no past tense before -아서; no command after -아서).

## Level
- Estimate the TOPIK level from where the point is usually taught (beginner 1–2,
  intermediate 3–4, advanced 5–6). When unsure, leave it empty as the app allows.
