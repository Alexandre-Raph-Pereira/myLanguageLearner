## Description

`-아/어도` says that the second clause is true **whatever the first clause says**: the expected consequence does not happen. It is the everyday "even if / even though".

1. **"Even if…"** (supposition): 비가 와도 갈 거예요 = I'll go even if it rains.
2. **"Even though…"** (real fact): 약을 먹어도 안 나아요 = even though I take medicine, I don't get better.
3. **`아무리 -아/어도` = "no matter how…"**: 아무리 바빠도 아침은 먹어요.

## Formation

- Stem with **ㅏ / ㅗ** + `아도`: 가다 → 가**도**, 보다 → 봐**도**, 좋다 → 좋**아도**.
- Other stems + `어도`: 먹다 → 먹**어도**, 마시다 → 마셔**도**, 바쁘다 → 바빠**도**.
- 하다 → 해**도**: 공부하다 → 공부해**도**.
- Irregulars follow the usual `-아/어` rules: 듣다 → 들어**도**, 덥다 → 더워**도**, 모르다 → 몰라**도**, 쓰다 → 써**도**.
- Past: `-았/었어도` (갔어도, 먹었어도), often for "even if … had" (알았어도 = even if I had known).
- Noun: `N이어도` after a consonant, `N여도` after a vowel: 학생**이어도**, 친구**여도**. (Colloquially `N이라도` is also heard here.)

## Examples

| Use           | Korean                                    | English                                                    |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Even if       | 비가 와**도** 축구를 할 거예요.           | We'll play football even if it rains.                      |
| Even if       | 몰라**도** 괜찮아요.                      | It's okay even if you don't know.                          |
| Even though   | 약을 먹**어도** 감기가 안 나아요.         | Even though I take medicine, my cold isn't getting better. |
| Even though   | 늦게 자**도** 일찍 일어나요.              | Even though I go to bed late, I get up early.              |
| No matter how | 아무리 바빠**도** 아침은 꼭 먹어요.       | No matter how busy I am, I always eat breakfast.           |
| Past          | 그때 알았**어도** 달라지지 않았을 거예요. | Even if I had known then, nothing would have changed.      |
| Noun          | 학생**이어도** 할인을 못 받아요.          | Even if you're a student, you can't get a discount.        |

## Similar · be careful

- **vs `-더라도`**: same meaning "even if", but `-더라도` stresses a hypothetical (often unlikely or extreme) case and sounds stronger and a bit more formal. `-아/어도` also works for real facts; `-더라도` usually doesn't (see the `-더라도` lesson).
- **vs `-지만` ("but")**: 비가 오지**만** 가요 = it's raining, but I'm going (two facts). 비가 와**도** 가요 = I go even if it rains (the rain doesn't change anything).
- **`-아/어도 되다` is a different grammar**: permission, "it's okay to…" (가**도 돼요**? = may I go?).
- **vs `-(이)라도`**: `-(이)라도` is a noun particle ("at least / even"); `-아/어도` is a connecting ending on verbs and adjectives.
